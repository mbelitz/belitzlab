        Generative AI tools (e.g., large language models, AI coding assistants) are increasingly part of research workflows. The following guidelines apply to their use in lab research, writing, and code:

        - **Disclosure:** If AI tools are used in a substantive way to draft text, generate code, or analyze data for a manuscript, thesis, or grant, this should be disclosed to co-authors and, where required, in the manuscript itself, consistent with journal and funder policies.
        - **Verification:** Lab members are responsible for independently verifying the accuracy of any AI-generated content, code, or analysis before it is used in a publication, presentation, or dataset. AI tools can be confidently wrong.
        - **Authorship:** AI tools are not authors. Responsibility for the accuracy and integrity of all work remains with the human researchers.
        - **Data privacy:** Do not input unpublished data, sensitive information, or confidential material (e.g., manuscripts under review, grant proposals) into AI tools without confirming the tool's data handling and privacy terms.
        - **Appropriate use:** AI tools can be useful for tasks such as literature discovery, code debugging, drafting boilerplate code, and improving writing clarity. They should support and not substitute for a lab member's own understanding of their research.

        This policy will be revisited periodically as norms and journal/funder policies evolve.
